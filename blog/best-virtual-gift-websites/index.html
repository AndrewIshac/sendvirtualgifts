The best virtual gift websites, compared honestly

By the SendLove team · October 12, 2026

Quick answer

It depends on the gift. For couples who want different kinds of gifts (letters, puzzles, countdowns, date plans), SendLove has the widest range. For flowers only, DigiFlower is strong. For a free option with no sign-up, AIskyla works well.

There are a lot of sites now that let you send a gift as a link. Some are great, some are basically an e-card with a new coat of paint. Here's what each of the better ones actually does, and who it suits.

Quick disclosure: this site is run by the people behind SendLove, which is one of the sites on this list. We've tried to be fair about where the others do a better job, and we say so when they do.

What makes a virtual gift website good?

Before the list, it helps to know what to look for, because the differences aren't obvious from the homepages. Almost every site promises "personalized" and "instant." That part is table stakes now.

What actually separates them:

  • Does the person have to sign up to open it? If your boyfriend has to create an account to see a love letter, half the magic is gone.
  • Can you add your own photos and words, or just pick from templates? The best gifts are the ones that could only be from you.
  • Is there something to do, or just something to read? A puzzle, a question, a date to accept. Interaction is what makes a link feel like a gift instead of a message.
  • Does it work on a phone without fuss? Most people open these from a text or WhatsApp. If it loads slowly or looks broken on mobile, that's the first impression.

With that in mind, here are the sites worth your time.

1. SendLove: best for couples who want more than one kind of gift

SendLove is built around relationships, and it has the widest range of gift types on this list. You can send a love letter (free), a photo album, a "why I love you" list, a countdown to the next time you see each other, a puzzle that reveals a photo, a date plan they can accept, a notebook, virtual flowers, and a "Make Them Say Yes" question where the No button runs away when they try to tap it.

The reason we'd put it first for couples is range. You're not stuck sending the same type of thing every time. A countdown works when you've booked a flight to see them. A puzzle works when you want something playful. A letter works when you have something real to say.

Good for: partners, long-distance couples, anniversaries, apologies, asking someone out.
Less good for: office or group gifts. It's very much a gift from one person to another.

2. Our GiftVerse: best if you want to "be" somewhere together

GiftVerse takes a different angle. Instead of a page, it builds a small 3D room you can both walk around in, with a cake to blow out together, a memory gallery, love letters and some couple games. It's designed for opening together on a video call.

It's a bigger, one-time purchase rather than a quick gesture, so it makes more sense for a birthday or anniversary than a random Tuesday. If you love the idea of a shared space, though, nothing else here does that.

Good for: a big occasion with a long-distance partner.
Less good for: quick, small surprises.

3. DigiFlower: best if you only want to send flowers

DigiFlower does one thing and does it well. You build a bouquet stem by stem (roses, peonies, tulips and so on), choose the wrap and card, add a little opening effect and some music, and send it as a link. The live preview while you build is genuinely nice.

If flowers are all you want, it's a strong pick. If you want a letter, photos or anything interactive alongside them, you'll need another site.

Good for: a "thinking of you" bouquet.
Less good for: anything beyond flowers.

4. AIskyla: best completely free option

AIskyla is free with no sign-up, which is hard to argue with. It offers animated gift boxes, love letters, birthday surprises, scratch cards and jigsaw puzzles, plus e-cards and small personal pages.

One thing to know: uploaded photos and music are deleted after 15 days, so it's better for a moment than a keepsake. If you want something they can come back to in a year, keep that in mind.

Good for: a quick, free surprise.
Less good for: something meant to be kept.

5. GiftFeels: best for occasion pages and extras

GiftFeels covers a wide range of occasions: birthday surprises, love letters, time capsules, digital scrapbooks, even wedding invitations with RSVP tracking. It's free to start, with paid add-ons like a personalized song.

It leans more toward "occasion page" than "relationship gift," which makes it a good fit for family events.

Good for: birthdays, family occasions, scrapbooks.
Less good for: small everyday gestures between partners.

6. SendVirtualGift: best for a quick 3D gift box

SendVirtualGift has 3D gift boxes, wax-sealed letters, birthday cakes, scratch cards and heart reveals, with no sign-up needed to create one. It's fast, and the gift box opening is fun the first time.

Good for: a fast, cute surprise.
Less good for: anything that needs a lot of your own photos or a longer story.

7. Gifft.me: best for a playful little quest

Gifft.me is free and ad-supported (you can pay to remove ads). Its gifts work like a small quest that ends with your personal message, and the site is available in many languages, which helps if the person you're sending to doesn't read English.

Good for: a light, funny surprise in another language.
Less good for: a serious or emotional message.

So which one should you pick?

Honestly, it depends on what you're trying to say:

  • "I love you, and here's why": SendLove's letter or "why I love you" list.
  • "I can't wait to see you": a countdown on SendLove.
  • "Let's spend this birthday together even though we can't": GiftVerse's shared room.
  • "Just thinking of you": DigiFlower or SendLove's flowers.
  • "I have zero budget right now": AIskyla, or SendLove's free love letter.

Whatever you choose, the site matters less than what you put in it. A specific memory and a few honest sentences will beat the fanciest animation every time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best website to send a virtual gift?

It depends on the gift. For couples who want different kinds of gifts (letters, puzzles, countdowns, date plans), SendLove has the widest range. For flowers only, DigiFlower is strong. For a free option with no sign-up, AIskyla works well.

Are virtual gift websites free?

Some are fully free, some are free to start with paid extras, and some charge per gift. SendLove's love letter is free; most other gift types are paid.

Does the person need an account to open a virtual gift?

On the sites in this list, no. The gift opens from a link in their browser.
