How to send wedding invitations on WhatsApp

By the SendLove team · October 30, 2026

Quick answer

Send each guest or family a link to an online invitation in a private WhatsApp chat, with a short personal message and a reply-by date. A link beats an image because you can update the details, greet guests by name and collect RSVPs in one list instead of chasing replies in chats.

WhatsApp is where most of your guests already are, which makes it a natural place to send your invitation. Here's how to do it so it feels personal, reaches everyone and doesn't bury you in replies.

Can you send a wedding invitation on WhatsApp?

Yes, and for a lot of couples it's now the main way invitations go out. Your aunt in Montreal, your college friends in Dubai and your neighbor two streets away all have WhatsApp open on their phones. A message lands in seconds, there's no postage, and you can see the two blue ticks when it's been read (unless they've turned read receipts off).

The question isn't really whether you can. It's how to do it so it still feels like a wedding invitation and not a forwarded meme. That comes down to three choices: what you actually send, how you send it to each person, and how you collect the replies.

What should you send: an image, a PDF or a link?

There are three common formats, and they behave very differently once they hit someone's chat.

Here's the thing about images and videos: once they're sent, they're frozen. If the venue changes or you push the start time by half an hour, you're sending a second message that says "Correction!" and hoping everyone sees it. A link can be updated, and the same link shows the new details.

The other big difference is replies. With an image, every RSVP comes back as a chat message, a voice note or a phone call, and you end up building a spreadsheet by hand at midnight. With an online invitation that has RSVP built in, guests tap a button and the answer lands in one list.

How do you send a wedding invitation on WhatsApp, step by step?

This is the method that works best for most weddings: an online invitation with a personal link for each guest or family.

  1. Finish your guest list first. Group it by household. Write down how many seats each household gets. This number matters later, so decide it now rather than in the chat.
  2. Make the invitation. Add your names, the date, the venue, and a reply-by date. If you have guests who read different languages, add those languages now.
  3. Preview it on your own phone. Send it to yourself first. Check that the date, time and address are right, and that the preview looks good in the chat.
  4. Create a personal link for each household. Type the guest's name and how many seats you're reserving for them.
  5. Send each link in a private chat with a short personal message (examples below).
  6. Track who has replied and send a gentle reminder a week or two before your reply-by date to anyone who hasn't.

Yes, sending one by one takes longer than blasting a group. For 150 households it might be an evening's work. But it's the difference between "you're invited" and "we invited you."

Should you use a WhatsApp group or a broadcast list?

Honestly, a big group is the option most couples regret. Everyone sees everyone's number, someone replies "Congratulations!!!" and sets off a flood of notifications, and your fiancé's boss ends up in a chat with your cousins. It also tells guests who else is invited, which isn't always what you want.

Broadcast lists are better because each person receives the message privately, as if you'd sent it just to them, and replies come back only to you. But there's a catch worth knowing: a broadcast only reaches people who have saved your number in their own contacts. If your partner's great-uncle hasn't saved you, the message quietly never arrives, and you won't get an error. That's a common reason a few guests "never got the invitation."

A broadcast also sends everyone the same text and the same link. You lose the personal greeting and the seat count per family.

Groups do have one good use: a small group for the wedding party or close family, created after invitations go out, for logistics like "the bus leaves the hotel at 4."

What should the WhatsApp message say?

Keep the chat message short. The invitation does the heavy lifting; the message just needs to sound like you and give them a reason to tap. A few you can borrow and adjust:

Warm and simple

Hi Rania! Karim and I are getting married on June 14 and we'd love for you to be there. Here's your invitation, please let us know by May 1 ❤️ [link]

A bit more formal (for older relatives)

Dear Uncle Sami and Aunt Mona, with great joy we invite you to our wedding. Your invitation is in the link below, with all the details. We would be honored by your presence. [link]

For friends abroad

Okay, it's official: we're getting married in Beirut next summer and it won't be the same without you. Everything's in here, including the date so you can look at flights early. [link]

Bilingual

Hi Tante Hoda! We're getting married and we'd love for you to celebrate with us. The invitation opens in Arabic for you. [link]

A few small things help. Put the link on its own line or at the end, so WhatsApp shows a clean preview. Use the person's name. Mention the reply-by date in the message as well as in the invitation; people skim. And if you're inviting a family, address the people who are actually invited, so no one wonders whether the kids are included.

How do guests RSVP to a WhatsApp invitation?

That depends entirely on what you sent. With an image, you're asking them to reply in the chat, which works for 30 guests and turns into chaos at 200. Replies come in as "yes we'll come," "inshallah," "how many can I bring?" and voice notes you need to listen to twice.

With an invitation that has a built-in RSVP, the guest taps accept or decline and picks how many people are coming, up to the seats you reserved. You get one list showing who's coming, who declined and who hasn't answered, and that third group is the one you chase.

If you're doing it by chat anyway, make life easier for yourself: ask a specific question ("Can you let us know by May 1 if you and Joe can make it?"), and keep a running list as replies come in, not at the end.

Is it okay to send wedding invitations only on WhatsApp?

For most guests, yes. A digital invitation isn't rude, and plenty of couples skip paper entirely. Where it gets delicate is with older relatives who expect a card in their hand, or with very formal families where paper is part of the tradition.

A sensible middle path: send WhatsApp invitations to almost everyone, and print a handful for grandparents and the elders who'd treasure them. You can also call those people first, then send the link so they have the details. Nobody is hurt by getting both a phone call and a link.

When should you send them?

Treat a WhatsApp invitation like a paper one. Common guidance is around six to eight weeks before a local wedding and earlier, often three months or more, for a destination wedding or when many guests are flying in. If you've already sent save-the-dates, the invitation can follow closer to the usual timeline.

Set the reply-by date about three to four weeks before the wedding. That gives you time to chase the stragglers and still hand a final number to the venue.

One small tip: avoid sending late at night. A wedding invitation at 1 a.m. reads more like a mistake than a celebration. Mid-morning or early evening on a weekend tends to get read properly.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Sending a giant image with tiny text. On a phone, the address becomes unreadable. Put the essentials in large type, or use a link that opens full screen.
  • Forgetting the reply-by date. Without one, people answer the week of the wedding.
  • Not saying who's invited. "The Haddad family" can mean two people or nine. A seat count removes the guesswork.
  • Relying on a broadcast list alone. Some guests won't receive it if they haven't saved your number.
  • Sending a correction to everyone. If you used a link you can update, just update it.
  • Sharing gift or bank details awkwardly in the chat. If you want to include a gift registry or transfer details, put them inside the invitation where guests can find them, rather than in the first message.

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude to send a wedding invitation on WhatsApp?

No. Digital invitations are common now, and WhatsApp is how many families already talk. For older relatives who expect paper, a phone call or a few printed cards alongside the link is a kind touch.

Should I send wedding invitations in a WhatsApp group?

Usually not. A large group shares everyone's number and fills with replies. Private chats or personal links work better, and a small group for close family is useful later for logistics.

Why didn't some guests receive my WhatsApp broadcast?

A broadcast message only reaches people who have saved your number in their contacts. Anyone who hasn't saved you won't get it, so send those guests a private message instead.

How do guests RSVP to a WhatsApp wedding invitation?

If you sent an image, they reply in the chat and you track answers by hand. If you sent an online invitation with RSVP built in, they tap accept or decline and you see every answer in one list.

What should I write in the WhatsApp message with the invitation?

Keep it short: the guest's name, that you're getting married, that you'd love them there, the reply-by date and the link on its own line.

When should I send WhatsApp wedding invitations?

Around six to eight weeks before a local wedding, and earlier for a destination wedding. Set the reply-by date three to four weeks before the day.
