Open when letters: what to write, with 12 examples you can copy

By the SendLove team · November 2, 2026

Quick answer

Open when letters are sealed notes labelled for a future moment, like "Open when you can't sleep." Write about 8 to 12, keep each one to a few sentences, and mix comfort, fun and celebration. Each letter should name the moment, add something only you could say, and give them one small thing to do.

You don't need a hundred ideas. You need a dozen good labels and some help getting the first line down.

Most lists of open when letters give you a few hundred envelope titles and then leave you alone with the hard part, which is the letter. So this one goes the other way. You'll get a sensible set to write, the rules to put in with them, and twelve short examples you can copy, change and sign.

What are open when letters?

They're a bundle of sealed letters, each labelled with the moment it's meant for: "Open when you can't sleep," "Open when you've had a win," "Open when you're mad at me." The other person keeps them and opens the right one when that moment turns up. Couples use them a lot during long distance, but they work just as well for a friend who's moved abroad, a kid leaving for university or someone heading off on a long posting.

What makes them different from a normal letter is timing. You're not there on the bad Tuesday, but something you wrote is. That's why the label matters as much as the words. It tells them you thought about this exact moment before it happened.

How many open when letters should you write?

Fewer than you think. Date Your Spouse says most people write about 8 to 15, and Shari's Berries suggests capping it at 10 to 12 so you don't get overwhelmed. Both are sensible. Ten letters you meant will beat forty you rushed, and by envelope twenty-five you'll start repeating yourself anyway.

If you only have one evening, write six. You can always add a second batch later, and honestly, a new envelope arriving three months in might be the nicest part.

Which letters should you write first?

Here's a balanced set of twelve. It's built so they'll get some use in the first few weeks and still have something left for later, which is the part most lists don't plan for. Mix the moods, because a bundle of only sad-day letters feels heavy to carry around.

Swap in whatever fits your life. If one of you has exams, write "Open the night before your exam." If a move is coming, write "Open on your first night in the new place." The more it could only be from you, the better.

What do you write in an open when letter?

Keep it short. A few sentences is plenty, and it's what makes writing a dozen realistic. Each letter only needs three things:

  1. Name the moment. Show you know what they're feeling, without fixing it. "So it's 2am and your brain won't stop."
  2. Add something only you could say. A real memory, an inside joke, a thing you've noticed about them.
  3. Give them one thing to do. Call me, go outside, put on that song, eat something. It gives the letter a job.

12 open when letter examples you can copy

Treat the brackets as the important part. Without your own details these are just nice words. With them, they sound like you.

1. Open when you miss me

I know. I miss you too, probably right now, probably while doing something boring like [washing dishes]. Close your eyes and go back to [that place we got lost in]. I was happy there, and I'm still that person. Now text me one word, any word, and I'll know you opened this.

2. Open when you can't sleep

Your brain's doing that thing again. Nothing it's telling you at this hour is reliable, you've said so yourself. Here's something true instead: I think about [the way you hum when you cook] more than you'd guess. Now put the phone down. Whatever it is will still be there at breakfast, and so will I.

3. Open when you've had a bad day

I'm sorry today was rubbish. You don't have to be fine about it or find the lesson in it. Tonight's only job is [your comfort food] and something stupid on TV. Tell me about it when you're ready, and tell me if you want advice or just someone on your side. I'm good at both, but I'm better at the second.

4. Open when you need a laugh

Remember [the waiter who called you by the wrong name all night, and you just answered to it]? I still think about that. I've told people about it, and I do the voice. You're welcome. Send me your worst impression of it right now, as a voice note. I'll be keeping it forever, against your wishes.

5. Open when you're bored

Good. I've got homework for you. Make me a playlist of five songs: one that sounds like us, one you're embarrassed to like, one for [our next road trip], one you think I'd hate and one you want me to really listen to. Send it over. I'll grade it harshly and we'll go through it on our next call.

6. Open when you've had a win

I knew it. I don't even need to know what happened, I knew it. I've watched you [stay up rewriting things nobody else would've noticed], and this is what that was for. Now stop whatever you're doing to play it down. Call me and tell me every detail, in order, and take your time.

7. Open on your birthday

Happy birthday, you. Here's what I want you to know this year: [one thing you've seen them become or get better at]. I wish I were there to embarrass you in a restaurant. Consider this a promise that I will, on [the next date you'll be together, or "the first one we can"]. Now go and be celebrated, and save me some cake in spirit.

8. Open when you're mad at me

You might be right. I wrote this on a good day so that I could say it before my pride gets involved: I'd rather sort this out than win it. You don't have to call yet. When you do, tell me what it felt like and I'll try to listen before I explain. I love you, and I'm probably being annoying.

9. Open when we've just had a fight

Fights feel bigger when we can't sit in the same room afterward. So here's a rule from a calmer version of me: nobody decides anything about us tonight. Sleep on it. We're still us in the morning. One of us will text first, and it's fine if it's you, and it's fine if it's me.

10. Open when you're wondering if this is worth it

It's okay that you're asking. I'd rather you ask than pretend. I'm not going to talk you out of the feeling. I just want you to have this in writing: when I picture [an ordinary future thing, like a Sunday morning at home], you're in it, and that's why I keep choosing this. Can we talk about what's been hard? Not to fix it fast, just so you're not holding it alone.

11. Open when you feel like you're not enough

You're going to read this and roll your eyes, so here are facts, not compliments. You [drove three hours to help your sister move]. You [remembered what I said about my dad months later and asked about it]. People who aren't enough don't do those things. Read this list twice, then text me one thing you did right today.

12. Open when we've just said goodbye

Airports are the worst. Everything's going to feel a bit flat for a day or two, and it doesn't mean anything's wrong. It just means the last few days were good. So here's the plan: eat something, sleep, and tomorrow we pick the next date. I'm already looking forward to [the thing you're doing next time].

What rules should you include?

Put a short "Open first" note on top. Date Your Spouse and Shari's Berries both suggest this, and Lasting the Distance adds a good one: ask them to tell you when they've opened a letter. Here's one you can use as it is:

Hi. These are for later, so here's how it works. Only open one when the label's actually true. One at a time. No peeking, and yes, I'll know. Tell me when you've opened one, but you don't have to tell me which. Keep them after, even the ones from when you were cross with me.

That "you don't have to tell me which" line is worth keeping. Sometimes they'll want to open "wondering if this is worth it" and aren't ready to have that talk yet. Letting them read it privately is part of the comfort.

Can you send open when letters online?

Yes, and if you're apart, it might be the better route. Posting a box abroad can take a while, and paper can't reach someone who's just landed. Digital letters also suit the way these get used, since the moment often hits when they're out, on a phone, nowhere near a shoebox.

The simplest setup takes about four steps:

  1. Write all your letters in one document first, so the voice stays consistent and you've got your own copy.
  2. Turn each one into its own link. On SendLove, the Love Letter is free and arrives as a letter in a sealed envelope that they open from the link, which keeps some of the ceremony of tearing one open. Any format that gives you a separate link for each letter will do.
  3. Make a single list with the envelope titles, each one linked to its letter, and send them that list with your "Open first" note. A shared note or a pinned message both work.
  4. Test every link before you send it, on a phone, because that's where it'll be opened.

One honest downside: a digital list runs on trust even more than an envelope does, since nothing physical gets torn. If you'd rather keep the surprise, you can hold on to the links and send one only when they text "I need the bad day one." It's a nice little ritual, though it stops working for the 3am letters if you're asleep, so you might hand over the comfort ones up front and hold back the rest.

Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

  • Writing all of them in one sitting. By letter nine you'll sound like a greeting card. Do three a night instead.
  • Making every letter about how much you miss them. They already know. Write about them, not your own sadness.
  • Using labels that could only be opened once a year. If half the bundle is holidays, it'll sit untouched for months. Keep most of them tied to everyday moods.
  • Promising things you can't control. "We'll be together by summer" reads badly if summer falls through. Promise what's yours to promise: that you'll call, that you'll be honest, that you'll pick the next date.
  • Treating a letter as a substitute for you. Every sad-day letter should end by pointing back to a real conversation. And if they're going through something serious, a letter's a kind extra, not the support they need. Call them, and encourage them to reach out to someone who can help in person.

A simple plan for this week

Tonight, pick your six most useful labels from the table and write down one real memory for each, nothing more. Tomorrow, write three letters. The night after, write the other three and your "Open first" note. By the weekend, you've got a set that's ready to seal or send, and you can leave the other six for a second batch when they least expect it.

Frequently asked questions

What are good open when letter topics for a long-distance relationship?

Start with moments that come up often: you miss me, you can't sleep, you've had a bad day, you've had a win, we've just had a fight and we've just said goodbye. Then add a couple that are specific to your relationship.

How long should an open when letter be?

A few sentences to a short page is plenty. Short letters are easier to write in batches, and they're easier to reread when someone's tired or upset.

Can I write open when letters for a friend or family member?

Yes. Keep the same structure and swap the romance for shared history: an old joke, something they taught you, a time they showed up for you. Labels like "Open when you've moved somewhere new" work well for friends and for kids leaving home.

Should I put anything inside the envelope?

It's optional. A photo, a small note or a song title is a nice touch if it means something to you both. Lasting the Distance suggests going easy on extras like confetti, so they don't end up in every envelope.

What should I do if they open all the letters at once?

Don't take it badly, because it usually means they really missed you. Write a smaller second batch, and put a couple of the new ones in your own hands so you can send them when the moment comes.

When should I give someone open when letters?

Just before you part is the classic moment, like the last day of a visit or the week before a move. If you're already apart, you can send them whenever a hard stretch is coming up, such as exams or a long gap between visits.
