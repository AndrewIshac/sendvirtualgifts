Save the dates in Christmas cards: should you combine them?

By the SendLove team · November 8, 2026

Quick answer

Yes, you can send save the dates with your Christmas cards, and it works best when your wedding is about 4 to 12 months away and only for people who are actually invited. Tuck a separate save the date inside the card so it doesn't get thrown out with the Christmas cards in January.

It sounds like a neat shortcut, and often it is. Here's how to tell if it suits your wedding, what to write, and the guest list mistake to avoid.

Can you send save the dates with Christmas cards?

Yes, and plenty of couples do. It's not a breach of etiquette. A save the date has one job: tell people early enough that they can protect the date, book time off and start looking at flights. If it arrives in the same envelope as your holiday card, it still does that job. Nobody serious thinks mailing them together is tacky.

The real question isn't "is it allowed?" It's "will it work for our wedding and our list?" Combining them goes well when three things line up: your wedding is a sensible distance away, your Christmas card list and your guest list are close to the same people, and the save the date stays easy to spot. If any of those is off, you're better off sending them separately, even if it means two mailings or two messages.

Here's how to tell which camp you're in, plus wording you can copy for each version.

Is your wedding date the right distance away?

The usual advice is to send save the dates about six to eight months ahead, and closer to a year for a destination wedding or a guest list with lots of people flying in. Christmas cards go out once a year, so the combo only makes sense if your wedding happens to fall in that window. Use this table to check.

Your wedding dateCombine with this Christmas card?Why
Less than 4 months away (a February or March wedding)NoYou're past save-the-date territory. Send the actual invitation instead.
4 to 6 months away (April to June)Yes, if you send it early in DecemberA little tight, so make the date impossible to miss and get invitations out soon after.
6 to 12 months away (June to December next year)Yes, this is the sweet spotRight in the normal window. Guests get the date before they plan their year.

If you only just got engaged and haven't booked a venue, don't send a save the date at all. Changing a date after people have booked flights is far worse than sending it a few months later. Put "We're engaged!" in the Christmas card and keep the date to yourselves until the deposit is paid.

Does your Christmas card list match your guest list?

This is the part most couples skip, and it's the one that causes real hurt feelings. Your holiday card list probably includes your hairdresser, the neighbours, your old boss and a few cousins you see once every five years. Your guest list might not.

A save the date is a promise. Anyone who gets one should get an invitation. So before you stuff a single envelope, sit down with both lists side by side and sort everyone into three groups:

  1. On both lists. These people get the Christmas card with the save the date inside.
  2. Christmas card only. These people get a plain Christmas card. No save the date, no "big news" line that hints at a wedding they're not coming to.
  3. Guest list only. People you're inviting but don't normally send a card to, like a partner's work friends. They still need a save the date, so send it on its own.

Group 3 is why the combo rarely saves as much effort as people hope. You'll almost always end up with a short separate mailing anyway. That's fine. Just plan for it instead of discovering it on December 20th.

Honestly, if your two lists overlap by less than about two thirds, just send them separately. Managing three versions of an envelope stops being a shortcut.

Separate card in the envelope, or one combined card?

You have two ways to do this, and they suit different people.

Option A: a separate save the date tucked inside the Christmas card

This is the safer choice. The save the date is its own card or magnet, with its own design, and it just shares an envelope. Guests can pin it to the fridge and throw out the Christmas card in January without losing your date. It also means you can design the save the date to match your wedding rather than the holidays.

Option B: one card that's both

A holiday photo card with "Save the date" and your wedding details printed right on it. It's charming and it's one thing to print, but it has a known weak spot: Christmas cards come down and go in the recycling. It's the worry couples raise most often about this option: a holiday-themed save the date gets thrown out with the rest of the cards. If you go this way, make the wedding details the biggest thing on the card, not a line under "Merry Christmas from the Smiths."

A simple rule: if your wedding is a winter or Christmas-themed wedding, Option B fits nicely because the festive look carries over. If it's a summer garden wedding, Option A keeps your save the date from looking like a holiday card.

What should a holiday save the date say?

Keep it short. The essentials are your names, the date, the city or town (so people can price travel), and a line saying a formal invitation will follow. Add a wedding website or "more details soon" if you have one. Everything else waits for the invitation.

The line inside the Christmas card (Option A)

If the save the date is a separate card, the Christmas card itself can carry a short handwritten line so it doesn't feel like an envelope of admin:

  • "We've tucked in a little something for next year. We can't wait to celebrate with you."
  • "Big year ahead! The date's inside. Hope you can make it."
  • "Merry Christmas from the two of us, and soon, the newlyweds."

Avoid writing "you're invited" anywhere on a save the date. It sounds like the invitation itself, and people will wait for nothing else or assume that's all they'll get.

What about guests who don't celebrate Christmas?

If your guest list includes Jewish, Muslim, Hindu or simply non-religious friends and family, "Merry Christmas" on a save the date can feel off, because the save the date is about your wedding, not your holiday. You don't need to overthink it. Either use "Season's greetings" or "Happy holidays" on the combined card, or use Option A and send the Christmas card only to people who'll enjoy it, with a neutral save the date for everyone.

The same goes for the new year. A "Happy New Year" save the date sent in the last week of December or the first week of January works for almost everyone, and it lands after the Christmas card rush, when your envelope is less likely to get lost in the pile. Some couples deliberately aim for early January for that reason.

When should you mail (or send) them?

The usual rhythm for Christmas cards is domestic cards in the post between late November and the first week of December, with international cards going out a couple of weeks earlier. That lines up well with save the dates, because the earlier yours arrives, the more time people have to plan.

A rough planner that works in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia:

WhenWhat to do
Early NovemberConfirm venue and date in writing. Merge your lists into the three groups above.
Mid-NovemberOrder or design the cards. Collect missing addresses (or phone numbers, if sending digitally).
Around 15 NovemberSend to anyone abroad by post.
Late November to first week of DecemberMail domestic combined cards. Send the separate save the dates to group 3 at the same time.
Mid-DecemberFollow up with anyone who hasn't seen it, especially older relatives and people overseas.

One note for Australia and New Zealand: Christmas falls in summer, a peak holiday and travel period. If your wedding is in December or January, that's a big ask, so give people as much notice as you can. A Christmas save the date a full year ahead is very reasonable for a wedding over the following summer holidays.

Post office cut-off dates change every year. Check your own postal service (USPS, Canada Post, Royal Mail or Australia Post) for this year's last recommended posting dates before you finalise your plan.

Can you send a save the date digitally at Christmas?

Absolutely, and for guests abroad it's often the smarter choice. A paper card to another country in December is at the mercy of the busiest mail weeks of the year. A message sent by email or WhatsApp arrives the same day. It also keeps the save the date out of the pile of Christmas cards altogether, which solves the "thrown out in January" problem.

A mixed approach works well: paper cards for the relatives who love opening them, and a digital save the date for younger guests and anyone overseas. If you're going digital, send it in a separate message from your holiday greeting. A wedding date buried under "Merry Christmas everyone!" in a family group chat will scroll away within an hour. Send each person their own message, and make the first line the date.

Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

MistakeFix
Sending a save the date to everyone on the Christmas listMerge the lists first. If it already happened, decide fast: either invite them, or call them personally and explain the guest list is small. Silence is the worst option.
The wedding date is in small print under the holiday greetingThe date should be the largest text on the card, or on its own card.
Addressing it to "The Smith Family" when only the adults are invitedAddress the envelope to exactly who's invited. Names on the envelope are how guests read the invitation.
Forgetting "formal invitation to follow"Add it. Without it, some guests will treat the save the date as the invitation and never expect more.
Sending before the venue contract is signedWait. A "we're engaged" line in the Christmas card is enough until the date is locked in.
Combining when the wedding is three months awaySkip the save the date and send the invitation instead.

A worked example

Say Megan and Chris are getting married in Toronto on 28 August 2027, about nine months after Christmas. Their Christmas card list has 60 households. Their guest list has 75 households.

  • 48 households are on both lists. They get a holiday card with a separate save the date tucked inside, mailed in the first week of December.
  • 12 households are Christmas card only (neighbours, a dentist, some old colleagues). They get a plain card with no wedding news beyond "We had a big year."
  • 27 households are guest list only, mostly Chris's university friends. They get a digital save the date by message the same week.
  • Six of the guests live in the UK. Their paper cards go out in mid-November, and they also get a message, just in case.

Total work: one design for the save the date, one Christmas card, one short message. Nobody gets a date for a wedding they're not invited to, and nobody important is left waiting on the post.

What comes after the save the date?

The formal invitation, usually six to eight weeks before the wedding, or a bit earlier when lots of guests are travelling. That's where the full details go: time, venue, dress code, RSVP date, and gift or registry information if you're including it. Frequently asked questions

Is it tacky to send save the dates with Christmas cards?

No. It's a common and practical choice. Just keep the wedding details easy to find and only include a save the date for people who will get an invitation.

How far in advance should a Christmas save the date go out?

Combine them when the wedding is about 4 to 12 months away, or up to 18 months for a destination wedding. Closer than 4 months, send the invitation instead.

Should the save the date be a separate card?

It's the safer option. A separate card or magnet can stay on the fridge after the Christmas cards are recycled, and it can match your wedding style rather than the holidays.

What if some people on my Christmas card list aren't invited?

Send them a plain Christmas card with no save the date. Anyone who gets a save the date should get an invitation.

Can I send a save the date by email or WhatsApp at Christmas?

Yes. It's often better for guests abroad. Send it as its own message, not inside a group holiday greeting where it gets lost.

Do I need to write 'formal invitation to follow'?

It's a good idea. It tells guests this is a heads-up, not the invitation itself, so they know more details are coming.
