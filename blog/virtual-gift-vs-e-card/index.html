Virtual gift vs e-card: what's the actual difference?

By the SendLove team · October 12, 2026

Quick answer

No. An e-card is a designed greeting you read. A virtual gift is built around your own photos and words, usually with something interactive like a puzzle, album or countdown.

Both arrive by link. Both can have a message and a bit of animation. So is a virtual gift just a fancy e-card? Not really, and the difference matters more than you'd think.

The short answer

An e-card is something you read. A virtual gift is something you open, do or keep.

That's the cleanest way we've found to explain it. An e-card is a greeting: a design, a short message, maybe a little animation. A virtual gift is built around an experience: solving a puzzle, flipping through an album, accepting a date, watching a countdown tick down.

How e-cards work

E-cards have been around since the early internet. You pick a design from a library, type a message, and email it. Some services, like Jacquie Lawson, are known for beautiful animated cards; others, like Punchbowl, focus on invitations and cards for events.

They're great at what they do. If you want to wish a coworker happy birthday or send your aunt something nicer than a text, an e-card is perfect.

What they're not great at is being personal. The card design is the same for everyone who picks it. Your message is a small part of it.

How virtual gifts work

A virtual gift flips that around. The design is a frame, and what you put inside it is the point: your photos, your words, your memories. And usually, there's something for the person to do:

  • Solve a puzzle to reveal a photo.
  • Scroll through an album of your relationship.
  • Pick a day and time for the date you planned.
  • Try to tap "No" while the button runs away from their finger.
  • Watch a countdown to the day you see each other.

That interaction is why a virtual gift feels like a gift and not just a message.

When to send an e-card

  • A coworker's birthday or farewell.
  • A holiday greeting to a big list of people.
  • Someone you're friendly with but not close to.
  • When you just want to say "hi, thinking of you" without making a big deal.

When to send a virtual gift

  • Your partner, especially if you're long distance.
  • A best friend going through something hard.
  • A parent or grandparent who'd love to see old photos.
  • When you want them to feel thought about, not just remembered.

Our take

Neither is better in general. They do different jobs. But if the person matters a lot to you, an e-card can feel a little thin. Ten more minutes and a few of your own photos turn it into something they'll actually keep.

Is a virtual gift the same as an e-card?
